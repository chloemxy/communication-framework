Empty Material panel: three options

What the panel has to do

  • Get a file in. Upload is the main action. Dropping a file and pasting text are the other two ways.
  • Say what files work, so nobody tries a format that fails.
  • Say the file stays in the browser. These are patient materials, so this matters.
  • Offer a sample for people who just want to try it.

What it no longer needs

  • A title. The panel is already called Material, and step 2 of the Guide says “Add your material”.
  • “Checks / Edit / Save”. The Guide on the right already explains the steps, and saving is explained where Export is.

What’s wrong with the current one

  • It’s a stock drop zone: a grey dashed box inside a panel inside the page (boxes in boxes), with a generic upload icon.
  • The two big buttons have the same size and weight, so it isn’t clear which comes first.
  • The samples look like plain text, so they’re easy to miss.
  • The notes underneath aren’t attached to anything and repeat the Guide.

Changes after review (all three options)

  • “Upload” became Open a file: to people handling patient materials, “upload” sounds like the file is sent to a server.
  • The privacy line now says plainly: “Nothing is sent anywhere. PRISM reads it in this browser.”
  • Every option fits a 340px panel without the privacy line dropping out of view.

Recommended: C. It has the clearest order and the biggest button, and its pictures show what the samples are. In the real panel I’d also accept a drop or Ctrl+V anywhere on the panel.

A. The blank page

Idea: the empty panel is the editor’s blank page, ready to type on. Open a file sits in a bar above it, and dropping anywhere on the page works. Good: fewest words; it matches the paper-on-a-desk look; typing needs no extra click. Risk: the upload button is the smallest of the three.
