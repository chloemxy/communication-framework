# PRISM logo brainstorm

The brief: "the site needs more character". The site bar shows only `PRISM` in Public Sans 600, with no mark and no favicon. This is a brainstorm. No existing file was changed.

- Page: `design/brainstorm/logo.html`. Each concept is shown at 160px, in a 48px site bar with Studio, Framework Compass and About, as 32px and 16px favicons with a browser tab, and on the dark desk (#E9EBEE on #121417). Checked at 375px with no horizontal scroll.
- Marks: `design/brainstorm/logo-svgs/`. Each mark is drawn on a 32-unit grid where 2 units equal 1px at 16px, so strokes land on whole pixels. They use `currentColor`, except `3-flagged-i.svg`, which is yellow plus currentColor. `3-flagged-i-ink.svg` is its one-colour version.

**The yellow rule (concept 3).** The current rule is that yellow marks only flagged words. Concept 3 breaks it, so it needs a rule change: "Highlighter yellow marks flagged words in the document, and appears in one fixed place in chrome: the logo." The logo's swipe is a slanted chisel shape that the document never uses, so it cannot be mistaken for a real flag. A milder option is to keep the site bar ink-only, using the ink outline version, and put yellow only in the favicon.

## Top 2

1. **Pilcrow P (6).** It gives the most character for the least disruption. It is one typographic quirk in a plain wordmark, it is an editor's mark that suits a text-proofing desk, it keeps the ink-only palette and the yellow rule untouched, and it works as a solid 16px favicon. Before adopting it, the glyph needs drawing to match Public Sans 600 weight exactly. It also needs a check that it stays distinct from the Penguin Press PP.
2. **Flagged I (3).** It is the most ownable and the most "PRISM". It borrows the product's own signature, the highlighter on a flagged word, and it is instantly recognisable in a tab. Its cost is the yellow-rule change described above.

Safe fallback: **Caret (2)**, the simplest mark at 16px with a clear editorial meaning.

## Similarity check

This is a design-similarity scan from web searches. It is **not** legal trademark clearance. Before adopting any mark or the name, run a proper trademark search: CIPO (Canada), USPTO (US) and the WIPO Global Brand Database. Use the relevant classes, at least 9 and 42 (software) and 44 (health).

**Name-level conflicts for "PRISM" in health (these matter more than the marks):**
- **PRISM, Program for Readability In Science & Medicine** (Kaiser Permanente Washington Health Research Institute). This is a plain-language and readability toolkit and training programme for health research materials, and it is still live. It is the same field and the same audience. This is the most serious conflict found, a name collision in the product's own niche. https://kpwashingtonresearch.org/about-us/capabilities/research-communications/prism
- **Prisma Health** (South Carolina health system). Its brand is explicitly built on light refracting through a prism. https://marketing.prismahealth.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/prisma_guidelines_v08a_CONDENSED.pdf
- **Prism Health** (Portland, OR, an LGBTQ+ health centre run by Cascade AIDS Project). https://www.capnw.org/news/2017/prism-logo-revealed
- Others: Prisma (ORM, prisma.io, a triangular "prism" symbol); the NSA PRISM surveillance programme, a strong negative association for a privacy-sensitive health tool; Oakley Prizm; Prysmian; and a USPTO PRISM mark (97546137) that is a pentagon of five triangles with the letters P-R-I-S-M (https://gleanmark.com/trademark/uspto-97546137).
